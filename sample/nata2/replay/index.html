What you're looking at

the cast
FLY — the real brain MaleCNS v1.0, the adult male fruit-fly connectome: 166,700 neurons, 25.6M connections. At every junction it views each exit's street photo through a simulated retina and turns the connectome's spike traffic into a direction score. This is the experiment: does the connectome walk?
COIN — no brain Picks a uniformly random exit at every step. The noise floor: a walker that does not beat COIN is not navigating.
GREEDY — no brain Always steps toward the exit that most reduces the straight-line distance to the goal. The sense-of-direction ceiling: how far FLY is from intent.

Honesty: what is real, what is engineered

Connectome weights are fixed anatomy, not a living fly. The decoder is an engineered mapping (DNp20 left/right differential with a DNpe017 gate), not a discovery of “walk neurons”. Dopamine and aversive pulses are engineered reinforcement signals, not modeled pain or pleasure. The visual adapter is an explicit display proxy; since v2 it honestly admits the goal-tinted column, measured inert against the live brain (per-junction corr −0.07, n=32) after the brightness veil proved direction-blind. Most likely the FLY line statistically resembles COIN — that is the honest result. Nothing here claims learned navigation unless the distance curve beats the random baseline reproducibly, and nothing here is neuroscience research.

The brain behind FLY
MaleCNS v1.0 connectome weights: fixed anatomy, 166,700 neurons and 25.6M directed connections (Google Research + HHMI Janelia). Not a living fly.
Engineered mapping inherited from Stonkfly: a DNp20 left/right differential with a DNpe017 spike gate. Not a discovery of “walk neurons”.
Reward signals
FLY’s moves are the connectome’s spike-decoded output on real imagery — this run: 64 brain consults, avg 4640 ms, p95 6074 ms.
Dopamine (PAM11) and aversive (PPL101) pulses are engineered reinforcement signals, not modeled pain or pleasure.
Vision
320×180 light-background frames of real Lisbon street imagery (Mapillary), photoreceptor-mapped.
The visual adapter is an explicit display proxy, not retinal physiology. Since v2 the frames carry a goal meter — a chart-ink column encoding each candidate’s distance reduction — engineered input, measured inert against the live brain (per-junction corr −0.07, n=32).
Outcome
GREEDY arrived at tick 23 (15.9 m from the goal); the other walkers did not arrive.
FLY stood closer to the nata than COIN on 117 of 400 ticks (29%), final distance 106.1 m vs COIN 117.7 m (ended 11.6 m closer), net −19.4 m vs COIN net −8.9 m — a direction-consistent but statistically weak edge, inseparable from random variation at tick resolution. The honest read: a real connectome making real decisions on goal-tinted frames still cannot turn visual salience into navigation, even on a corridor GREEDY walks in 23 steps.
Claims
Every logged step happened on real imagery through the real connectome, or through the honest baselines.
No learned navigation is claimed on either run. Nothing here is neuroscience research or investment advice.
